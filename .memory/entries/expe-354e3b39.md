Corrects a recommendation I made before running it, and saves ~45 min per future dream run.

I suggested the 64-step dreams might be UNDERTRAINED and that raising --dream-steps to 300-500 was plausibly the single biggest cheap win. That is wrong for the projected path.

EVIDENCE (/tmp/skull_proj_v040, 48x24 control, --project-in-loop, --dream-volume 0.4, 300 steps, 3395 s / ~56.6 min):
The CLIP loss fell from ~16 to ~0.45 by step ~50, then oscillated in a 0.42-0.43 band for the remaining 250 steps. Final 0.427 vs a late low of 0.424. Plateaued, not still descending.

So ~80-100 steps is sufficient for the projected dream; 300 spent 45+ extra minutes inside the noise band. Size future sweeps accordingly -- this matters because a 4-config sweep at 300 steps is ~4 hours, versus well under an hour at 100.

CAVEAT: this curve is from the PROJECTED path. The unprojected 64-step runs were not instrumented with a loss curve (the curve is only saved now, as part of the same change), so undertraining of the gray dreams remains untested. Do not generalize the plateau to the default path without measuring it.

MECHANISM SETTINGS THAT PRODUCED IT, for reproducibility: squash sigmoid(4*(x-0.5)) so gradients survive outside [0,1]; volume_weight 1000; projection_sigma 2.0; beta annealed 1 -> 8; scaffold_source passthrough with scaffold_mean 0.3999 against a 0.4 target.

RESULT QUALITY (separate from the timing point): clip loss 0.427 is WORSE than the gray 48x24 at 64 steps (0.390), and the image is a filled blob with two holes rather than a skull. Binarity, volume pinning, and passthrough all worked mechanically; the shape did not survive the ink budget. Prompt was the bare 'human skull' with no style wrapper, held fixed deliberately so the projection change stayed isolable.

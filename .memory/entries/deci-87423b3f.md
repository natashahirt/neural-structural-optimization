SUPERSEDES the core premise of the parent E1b plan. The parent argued CLIP cannot supply large-scale shape coherence (bag-of-patches, no global-shape term) and therefore needed non-structural shape regularizers. Viewing script/resources/results/clip_saliency_compliance/human_skull_projected_full/physical_density.png refutes that.

WHAT THE IMAGE SHOWS: a large, detailed, unmistakable skull - cranium, orbits, nasal aperture, teeth - occupying essentially the whole 128x256 domain. One skull at global scale. CLIP already produces the global shape we were designing an architecture to obtain.

WHY THE EARLIER DIAGNOSIS WAS WRONG: it generalized from 'butterfly wing venation', which is a TEXTURE prompt. A texture prompt producing texture is correct behaviour, not a CLIP limitation. The user identified this confound. Object prompts behave qualitatively differently.

THE ACTUAL FAILURE, VISIBLE IN THE IMAGE: the skull is rendered in fine gray HATCH lines (pencil-shading morphology) while thick black members - vertical columns and branching struts - form a load path that runs through it and ignores it. This is clip_compliance_overlap 0.1781 and gradient_cosine -0.0728 made visual. Not a shape problem, not a scale problem: a density/hatching problem.

KEY UNRUN EXPERIMENT: every projection run on disk uses scales:['global'] only - verified across all 10 dirs in script/resources/results/clip_saliency_compliance/ (human_skull_projected, human_skull_projected_full, butterfly_wing_venation_projected all global; the rest have no projection block). The all-scales projection with sigma at member width, which the user explicitly asked for, HAS NEVER BEEN RUN. expe-54faec24 diagnosed its own failure in exactly these terms: 'Closing ink at the sculptural scale does not prevent CLIP from hatching the motif once detail scales unlock.' The hatch lines are thinner than a structural member, so a min-feature-size constraint at storey/member scale would make them unrepresentable.

NEW FRAMING (user's): we were conflating 'global skull' with 'structure' too fast. The gray ink may be a symptom of ONE field being asked to be a skull and a structure simultaneously - physics takes the black material for load paths, leaving CLIP to draw with the remainder. Separate the stages: let CLIP make the skull with no FEA competing for the field, then hand it downstream as a sketch prior via the proven stage6_sketch_on_golden path.

WHAT SURVIVES FROM THE PARENT PLAN: the sketch interface (reuse the proven downstream, human sketches as positive control); letterboxing rather than square-cropping or squashing, to keep the domain aspect the structural problem dictates; resolution as the primary feature-size control; connectivity in the objective (per expe-41216fc2's 14 disconnected components).

WHAT IS DROPPED: the perimeter/TV term (see sibling note-b2a8ce44), and the premise that global shape must be manufactured by added losses.

OPEN QUESTION the user raised: global-only or multi-scale dream? whole_building_dream_config currently ASSERTS motif_scale_fracs is empty (clip_dream_layout.py:300-303), so the dream is global-only by construction. Multi-scale plus all-scales projection, with no FEA, is the untested combination most likely to give a detailed skull built from members rather than hatch.
